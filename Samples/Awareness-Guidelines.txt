PHISHING AWARENESS GUIDELINES
=============================

HOW TO IDENTIFY A PHISHING EMAIL
--------------------------------

1. Check the Sender Address
Look carefully at the complete email address, not just the displayed sender name.
Be suspicious of unfamiliar domains, misspellings, or domains designed to imitate trusted organizations.

2. Look for Urgency
Phishing emails often create pressure by using deadlines such as:
"Act within 24 hours"
"Your account will be suspended"
"Immediate verification required"

3. Watch for Threatening Language
Messages that threaten account closure, financial loss, legal action, or security problems may be using fear to influence the recipient.

4. Check Links Before Clicking
Hover over links to inspect the destination.
Be cautious when the displayed link does not match the expected organization's official domain.

5. Never Share Sensitive Information
Legitimate organizations generally should not ask users to provide passwords, OTPs, PINs, or complete payment credentials through suspicious email links.

6. Be Careful With Attachments
Do not open unexpected attachments, especially executable files or documents received from unknown senders.

7. Check the Greeting and Message Quality
Generic greetings, unusual wording, spelling mistakes, and inconsistent branding can be warning signs.

8. Check Reply-To Information
The Reply-To address may be different from the visible sender address.
An unexpected or suspicious Reply-To address should be treated as a warning sign.

9. Verify Through an Official Channel
If an email claims there is a problem with your account, open the organization's official application or manually enter its known website address instead of using the email link.

10. Check Email Authentication When Available
Security teams can review SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results in the email headers.
Authentication failures can provide additional evidence that a message may be suspicious.


EMPLOYEE DO'S
-------------

DO verify suspicious requests through an official website or application.

DO inspect the sender's complete email address.

DO hover over links before clicking them.

DO report suspicious emails to the organization's security or IT team.

DO verify unexpected payment, password-reset, or account-change requests using a trusted communication channel.

DO use multi-factor authentication where available.

DO delete suspicious messages after reporting them according to organizational policy.


EMPLOYEE DON'TS
---------------

DON'T click suspicious links.

DON'T open unexpected attachments.

DON'T provide passwords, OTPs, PINs, or banking information through email links.

DON'T reply to suspicious messages.

DON'T trust an email simply because the displayed sender name looks familiar.

DON'T ignore browser or security warnings.

DON'T install software requested by an unexpected email.

DON'T bypass organizational security controls to access a suspicious website.


WHAT TO DO IF YOU CLICKED A SUSPICIOUS LINK
-------------------------------------------

1. Stop entering information immediately.

2. Close the suspicious webpage.

3. Report the incident to the organization's IT or security team.

4. If credentials were entered, change the affected password using the legitimate website or application.

5. If the same password was reused elsewhere, change it on those services as well.

6. Enable multi-factor authentication where available.

7. Follow the organization's incident-response instructions.


SIMPLE PHISHING ATTACK FLOW
---------------------------

FAKE EMAIL
    ↓
URGENCY OR FEAR
    ↓
SUSPICIOUS LINK / ATTACHMENT
    ↓
USER INTERACTION
    ↓
CREDENTIAL OR INFORMATION THEFT RISK


KEY TAKEAWAY
------------

Phishing attacks rely heavily on social engineering.

Before clicking a link, opening an attachment, or providing sensitive information:

STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT
